Two serious security flaws in PHP, the software behind many of the world's websites

PHP is the software that WordPress and millions of other websites run on. A fix came out on 21 October 2026. Government cyber agencies have told organisations to install it now; the two US agencies add: take websites offline if you cannot. Phones and personal computers are not affected.

The core facts, and the evidence for each

  • confirmed two or more original sources agree
  • reported one party says so; nobody has contradicted it
  • disputed sources disagree
  • unverified nobody has been able to check it
  1. confirmed

    There are two flaws. Both are in PHP itself, underneath every website that uses it, and both are rated critical: 9.2 out of 10.

    CVE record CVE record php.net More →

  2. confirmed

    Someone anywhere on the internet, with no password, can use them against a website that has not installed the fix, and make its server run their own commands. Researchers proved this on the most traditional way of running PHP; for other setups it is likely but less certain.

    PHP PHP PHP Foundation More →

  3. confirmed

    The fix is in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, released on 21 October 2026. Websites on PHP 8.1 or older have no ready-made fix.

    php.net php-src More →

  4. reported

    The weak spot was first noticed in 2024 and left alone, because nobody could show it was dangerous. In August 2026, three research teams using AI tools showed that it was, and told the PHP project.

    PHP Foundation Calif Wiz More →

  5. confirmed

    The plan was to announce the fix on 22 October. Word leaked out on 20 October, so it was released a day early.

    PHP Foundation Daubois More →

  6. reported

    Attackers are trying, in large numbers: one internet security company reports millions of attempts, and Amazon reports a decoy server hit four days before the announcement. No named organisation has said it was broken into through these flaws, as of 09:35 UTC on 25 October, and the US government's official list of flaws known to be used in attacks did not include them when last checked.

    Matthew Prince, Cloudflare CEO on… THN Paperjam CISA CISA More →

  7. confirmed

    Every agency says install the fix. They disagree on what to do if you cannot: the US says take the website offline, the UK lists that as a last resort, several European agencies say cut it off from the public internet, and some say nothing about it.

    CISA NCSC UK CERT-EU ACSC More →

  8. confirmed

    The headline '70% of the Internet' is not what it sounds like. It is roughly the share of websites whose server-side language is known that use PHP, which is what the same company's own article says; nobody has measured how many are actually at risk.

    Wiz Wiz on X Cloudflare More →

What nobody knows yet

  • Whether any website was actually broken into before it installed the fix.
  • Whether the fix is complete. A report on 21 October suggested it may not be; the PHP security team replied on the mailing list on 22 October, and this site will settle the question once that reply is saved here. Separately, on 23 October PHP published an advisory for another flaw, CVE-2026-115560, which affects fully patched releases and needs a rare session setting (reported by Cloudflare, SOCRadar and The Hacker News).
  • Who leaked the news on 20 October, and where.
  • How many websites went offline, and why. The reports this site holds range from a single site to a whole government's platform; a community tracker lists about fifty sites that went dark and came back. Nobody has counted them all.
  • Where the 70% figure comes from. None of the pages that use it says how it was measured.
