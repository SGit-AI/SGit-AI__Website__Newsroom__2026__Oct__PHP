PHP news · October 2026 · Developing · last checked 22 October 2026, 11:34 UTC

Two serious security flaws in PHP, the software behind many of the world's websites

PHP is the software that WordPress and millions of other websites run on. A fix came out on 21 October 2026. Government cyber agencies have told organisations to install it now, or to take websites offline if they cannot. Phones and personal computers are not affected.

What is happening nowThe briefing for youExplain it from the start

You may also see this called php2shell, or by its two official reference numbers, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619.

Right now

As of 22 October 2026, 11:34 UTC, the time of the latest page this site saved.

  • A fix has been available since 21 October. Organisations that run websites on PHP are installing it. php.net
  • Government cyber agencies in the US, the UK, the EU and elsewhere say: install the fix now, and take a website offline if you cannot. CISA NCSC UK CERT-EU NSA Cyber on X
  • Attackers are trying to use the flaws, in large numbers. No organisation has said it was broken into. Matthew Prince, Cloudflare CEO on… CISA
  • Some organisations took websites offline as a precaution, among them Dutch government services such as the DigiD login. Rijksoverheid / NCSC-NL BBC News

  1. confirmed

    There are two flaws. Both are in PHP itself, underneath every website that uses it, and both are rated critical: 9.2 out of 10.

    CVE record CVE record php.net More →

  2. confirmed

    Someone anywhere on the internet, with no password, can use them against a website that has not installed the fix, and make its server run their own commands. Researchers proved this on the most traditional way of running PHP; for other setups it is likely but less certain.

    PHP PHP PHP Foundation More →

  3. confirmed

    The fix is in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, released on 21 October 2026. Websites on PHP 8.1 or older have no ready-made fix.

    php.net php-src More →

  4. reported

    The weak spot was first noticed in 2024 and left alone, because nobody could show it was dangerous. In August 2026, three research teams using AI tools showed that it was, and told the PHP project.

    PHP Foundation Calif Wiz More →

  5. confirmed

    The plan was to announce the fix on 22 October. Word leaked out on 20 October, so it was released a day early.

    PHP Foundation Daubois More →

  6. reported

    Attackers are trying. One internet security company reports millions of attempts; Amazon reports a decoy server was hit four days before the announcement.

    Matthew Prince, Cloudflare CEO on… THN More →

  7. confirmed

    No organisation has confirmed a break-in, and the US government's official list of flaws known to be used in attacks did not include these two when last checked.

    CISA CISA More →

  8. confirmed

    Every agency says install the fix. They disagree on what to do if you cannot: the US says take the website offline, the UK lists that as a last resort, several European agencies say cut it off from the public internet, and some say nothing about it.

    CISA NCSC UK CERT-EU ACSC More →

  9. confirmed

    The headline '70% of the Internet' is not what it sounds like. It is roughly the share of websites built with PHP, which is what the same company's own article says; nobody has measured how many are actually at risk.

    Wiz Wiz on X Cloudflare More →

What nobody knows yet

Stated, not papered over. This site will update each one when the evidence does.

  • Whether any website was actually broken into before it installed the fix.
  • Whether the fix is complete. A report on 21 October suggested it may not be; the PHP project has not answered it publicly yet.
  • Who leaked the news on 20 October, and where.
  • Exactly which websites went offline, and why. Most of the list comes from one news live page and has not been confirmed by the organisations named.
  • Where the 70% figure comes from. None of the pages that use it says how it was measured.
