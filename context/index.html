In context: php2shell against the flaws that came before

This page v5 written in site v0.14.30 on 24 October 2026 · history and changes, 4 earlier

In plain English How php2shell compares with famous past flaws such as Heartbleed and Log4Shell, using the official record of which ones were exploited.

The editor has worked in security long enough to remember Kaminsky's DNS flaw and Heartbleed, and asked whether this one is as big as the morning's coverage made it. The honest way to answer is a table: ten landmark flaws, each with its CVE record fetched into the vault and its row in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, captured on 22 October. The catalog is the one public, dated, machine-readable record of 'this was exploited', and php2shell is not in it yet.

Comparators chosen by the editor's memory and the KEV catalog, not by any ranking. Dates are from the CVE records fetched by this site into the vault and from the CISA KEV catalog captured on 22 October; the 'exploited by ransomware' column is CISA's field.

What the comparison says

The site's reading of the table above. Positions, with the evidence attached.

  1. Severity is not exposure. php2shell's 9.2 is a property of the bug; Heartbleed's 'everyone' and Log4Shell's 'everywhere' were properties of deployment. The comparators show both kinds of big: a bug in a runtime that everything sits on (Log4Shell, Shellshock, php2shell) and a bug whose real exposure was a narrow configuration (CVE-2019-11043, Spring4Shell, CVE-2024-4577 on Windows CGI). Which kind php2shell is depends on how many of the 1.57 million unpatched hosts Censys counted are mod_php on x86_64 with the default settings, a number nobody has published.
  2. Urgency before disclosure was institutional, not public. The PHP project had the fix on 18 October and sat on it for a coordinated release; Cloudflare had rules live nine hours early; Amazon had honeypot hits four days early; agencies published within the hour. That is the Kaminsky model working as designed, right up to the point it leaked. The public alarm came with the agencies' offline advice on the morning of 22 October, which is why the editor's question, 'why was the alarm only raised when it went public', has a factual answer: because the embargo held for everyone who was inside it.
  3. The KEV test. CISA lists a CVE when it has reliable evidence of exploitation in the wild. Log4Shell and MOVEit were listed the day they were published; CVE-2024-4577 in three days. php2shell was not listed after one day, with Amazon saying it is 'broadly exploited' against honeypots and NCSC NZ 'aware of reports'. Either the evidence has not reached CISA's bar, or attempts against sensors are not the same as exploitation of victims. This site will record the day it is added, if it is.
  4. What is new is not the bug class but the discovery pipeline. The OOM-bailout pattern was seen in 2024 and shelved because nobody could prove exploitation. Three teams with model assistance proved it inside three weeks of each other. The historical comparison that matters is therefore not Heartbleed but the gap between 'theoretically exploitable' and 'proven', which used to be months of expert work and may now be days.

The signals that would move php2shell into the catalog are on the exploitation page; what was taken offline, and by whom, is on impact.
