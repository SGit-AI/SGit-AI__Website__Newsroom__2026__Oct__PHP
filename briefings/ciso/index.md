# What is our exposure, what is our evidence we were not hit, and what do I tell the board?

R
The answer in thirty seconds

The flaw is in the PHP runtime, so exposure is every internet-facing system running PHP, including vendor appliances. Review logs from 17 October (the earliest reported attempt), not from the fix. Attempts are reported at scale; no named victim is confirmed. Patch first, isolate where you cannot.

[Read the site as Robin →](../../impact/index.html)

Exposure, evidence of non-compromise, and the board message. Three facts carry this briefing: the bug is in the runtime so your application code is not the question; attempts are in the millions at one CDN but no named victim is confirmed anywhere; and every agency says patch first, with offline as the fallback not the reflex.

## What to decide or do
- Exposure is a list, not a percentage: every internet-facing system serving PHP, with SAPI, version and what sits behind it. Vendor appliances and hosting panels count.
- Evidence of non-compromise means log review from 17 October (Amazon's earliest attempt), not from the 21st, and host checks for shells spawned by PHP workers, not just WAF hits.
- The board line: a critical runtime flaw, patched on day one where we could, isolated where we could not, no evidence of compromise as of today, review continuing. Do not quote 70%.
- Edge rules were live hours before disclosure; if you are behind a major CDN you had a stopgap you did not have to ask for. It is not a control you can rely on.
- Expect a follow-up release: a report that the first fix is incomplete reached the security list within two hours.

## What is still open
- Who first leaked the CVE IDs on 20 October, and where. The PHP Foundation does not say; a PHP security team member points to an anonymous Hacker News post, and Notebookcheck and Nguoi Quan Sat repeat that account. No copy of the post is in the vault.
- Whether the deleted Calif video is the one now embedded on Calif's own write-up. Calif's page does not mention the leak.
- Whether any site was actually compromised before it was patched or taken offline. Every claim of exploitation so far is of attempts, probes or honeypot hits, and the named cases do not state php2shell as the cause. Authorities in Singapore and Luxembourg now ask operators to look for traces; the answer will come from those searches, if it comes.
- Which sites were really taken offline, and why. The list now draws on more captured reports (Germany's hospitals and hosts, Morocco, Romania, Japan), but most are press accounts; few come from the organisations themselves (the Dutch government, Latvia's agency, Weird Gloop, the Charité's spokesman as quoted). A community tracker counts 51 listed and 50 back.
- Whether the 8.5.12 fix is complete. A Fediverse report of a third bug or incomplete fix reached oss-security on 21 October; PHP reportedly told Canonical it is unrelated. Nothing from PHP on the list yet.
