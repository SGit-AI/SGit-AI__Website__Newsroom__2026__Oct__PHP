The bottom line

  1. Fix itUpdate PHP to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35, and restart it. Installed is not running.confirmed
  2. No panic, no complacencyAttackers are trying in large numbers; no organisation has confirmed a break-in.reported
  3. Decide offline nowIf a system cannot be patched today, name who can take it offline, and when. The agencies disagree, so the decision is yours.confirmed

How it unfolded

  1. 2024An audit spots the out-of-memory weakness. Nobody can show it is exploitable, so it is left.
  2. Aug 2026Three teams using AI turn it into working attacks within three weeks and report them to PHP.
  3. 20 OctThe secret leaks: the CVE numbers circulate two days early, with a wrong severity score.
  4. 21 OctPHP releases the fix a day ahead of plan. Firewall vendors already have rules live.
  5. 22 OctAgencies warn: update, or take systems offline. Sites go dark as a precaution.

What nobody knows yet

  • Whether any website was actually broken into before it installed the fix.
  • unverified Whether the fix is complete. A report on 21 October suggested it may not be; the PHP project has not answered it publicly yet.
  • Who leaked the news on 20 October, and where.
  • Exactly which websites went offline, and why. Most of the list comes from one news live page and has not been confirmed by the organisations named.
  • Where the 70% figure comes from. None of the pages that use it says how it was measured.

CISA oss-security BBC News

What to do, by who you are

You run a website

  • Ask your host which PHP version is serving your site now, and when it was restarted
  • Ask whether anything still runs PHP 8.1 or older

You run the servers

  • Patch and restart every PHP process
  • No patch today: the temporary settings, an edge rule, then offline
  • Look for workers spawning shells

You own the risk

  • Name who can take a system offline
  • Ask for evidence you were not hit
  • Plan for 8.1 and older
