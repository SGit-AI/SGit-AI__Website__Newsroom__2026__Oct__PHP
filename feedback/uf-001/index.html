problem
Newsroom articles lack a clear TOC so it's difficult to get a feel for what they contain and to navigate them

UF-001.4 · problem reproduced medium

Confirmed. Neither house story measured has a table of contents: 'Where php2shell stands' has 10 sections and about 2,100 words, 'Patch at the speed of Metasploit' 9 sections and about 2,950 words. The reader has to scroll to learn what the article covers.

Evidence. Measured on 0.8.1 in Chromium: no .toc element on articles/where-php2shell-stands/ or articles/patch-at-the-speed-of-metasploit/; h2 counts 10 and 9.

Recommend. An 'On this page' list built from the h2s, at the top of every article and of every page with four or more sections; collapsed to one line on phones so it does not cost the first screen (F001). Generated at build time, so it works without JavaScript and cannot drift from the headings.

problem
The fact that only the first use of a specialist word has a tooltip can be limiting, as the reader might have missed where the first occurrence was or has jumped forward or might use a refresher on the term down the page. I understand that underlying every word keeps the page very busy for things that potentially the reader don't care about or has learned. Not sure what the best solution is, but the way it's built now tends to reduce the power of the glossary. I would be inclined to try keeping underlining the specialist words the first time they appear only, but keeping the tooltip active for every occurrence.

UF-001.7 · problem reproduced medium

Confirmed, and the reader's proposal is a good one. On /flaws/, 'PHP' appears 41 times and 'CVE' 20 times, and each has a card on its first use only. A reader who arrives through an anchor, or jumps to a section, never meets the first use.

Evidence. Counted on flaws/index.html on 0.8.1: PHP 41 occurrences, CVE 20, CVSS 3, CWE 2; one card each.

Recommend. As the reader proposes: the first use stays underlined; every later use carries the same card with no underline (a dotted underline only on hover or focus), so the page stays calm and the card is always there. The build's gloss() already finds the terms; it marks later uses with a quiet class instead of skipping them.

problem

Confirmed by reading /collaborators/: the feedback form is per ask. The Feedback button on every page (v0.8.0) is per page, but it needs a key from an invitation, and no invitation has been sent yet (REALITY.md), so this reader had no way to send page-level feedback except email. bug
The text on the "at a glance" tiles on the front page overlaps (screenshot)

UF-001.9 · bug reproduced medium

Reproduced in every theme and at most widths. The tile 'Websites taken offline' shows '10 organisations' in the 1.35rem serif; 'organisations' is wider than the tile (134 to 142px) and the stylesheet forbids breaking it (.tile b {overflow-wrap: normal}), so it runs 3 to 11px into the next tile. The other tiles fit.

Evidence. Measured on 0.8.1 in Chromium in all six themes at 320, 600, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440px wide: overflow of 5, 9, 4, 11, 3 and 3px. Fits at 360, 390, 414 and 820px.

Recommend. Put the number alone in the large type ('10') and move the unit into the small line ('organisations took websites offline, as a precaution'), as the other tiles already do with 'Yes' and 'Not yet'. A guard for the next long word: .tile b {overflow-wrap: anywhere}. The gate's browser check could flag any element wider than its tile.

Who can act: Story site agent, with dev@php.newsroom · friction F015

bug
The "open the glossary -->" link from tooltip is unreachable (screenshot)

UF-001.10 · bug reproduced medium

Reproduced, and it is unreachable by design: the card is drawn with pointer-events: none, it closes as soon as the pointer leaves the word, and 'Open the glossary' is plain text, not a link. The card promises an action it cannot give. Clicking the word itself does open the glossary, but nothing says so. On a touch screen the card cannot be opened at all without following the link.

Evidence. On flaws/index.html on 0.8.1 in Chromium: hovering 'PHP' shows the card (no links inside it, pointer-events none); moving the pointer to 'Open the glossary' closes it. Code: the showTip() and mouseout handler in the site script written by build_sitejs(), and .tip in assets/site.css.

Recommend. Quick and honest: change the line to 'Click the word for the glossary entry'. Proper: make the line a real link to the term's glossary entry, let the card take the pointer (pointer-events: auto) and keep it open for a short grace period while the pointer moves from the word to the card; on touch, the first tap opens the card and the link inside it goes on. Keyboard: focus already opens it; Escape closes it.

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand.
