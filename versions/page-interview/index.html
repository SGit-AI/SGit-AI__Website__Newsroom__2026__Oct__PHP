# The editor, interviewed: Dinis Cruz on why this site exists

Dinis Cruz has worked in application security for a long time and runs the SGit Newsroom. He started this site on the morning of 22 October 2026 with two voice memos and kept steering it through the day. The questions below are the ones a reader would ask; the answers are his.

How this was made: the questions are written by the agent that builds the site; every answer is the editor's own words, quoted verbatim from his voice memos and voice notes of 22 October as transcribed, including the hesitations. Nothing was paraphrased. A live interview is pending, and its questions are at the foot of the page.

Okay, so there's a big vulnerability flying around at the moment, and I would like to use this as a good case study for what we can do in the newsroom. But it's also a good way to explore other ways to visualise and to map a lot of this stuff.

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand. One of the SGit Newsroom's story sites: up from here are October 2026, the year and the newsroom, whose own section on the story is here. Part of the sgit.ai network.
