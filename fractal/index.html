From a line of code to a stakeholder

One graph for the whole story. At the bottom, the 359 changed hunks of the php2shell fix, each a line range in php-src; above them the functions and files they sit in, the parts of the fix, the two flaws; then the claims and events that talk about them and the sources those cite; then the story arcs those claims carry, the risks the arcs describe, and at the top the fifteen kinds of reader who face those risks. 898 nodes, 1233 edges. Every node opens into its own neighbourhood: walk it, from the top or from a line.

The top three layers and the joins from a claim to a flaw or a part of the fix are written by the librarian in data/arcs.json; everything else is derived by the build from files the site already publishes. The assembled graph is data/fractal.json.

What is joined, and what is not

  • 359 of 359 changed hunks reach a stakeholder by walking up the graph.
  • 215 of 359 reach a named part of the fix (a soft limit, an unwind, globals reset and so on). The rest reach the fix only as "the commit touched this area": the review joined nine files to what the fix does, and the other files of the engine wait for that join.
  • 15 of 16 stakeholders reach a changed line by walking down.
  • Not here yet: the 352 files outside the engine and bundled extensions the fix also touched (mostly tests), and the lines of the PHP 7.4 backport.
  • Not here by design: the vulnerable code before the fix. The graph maps the lines the fix wrote, never a trigger path (the site's security requirements, AGENTS.md).

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand.
