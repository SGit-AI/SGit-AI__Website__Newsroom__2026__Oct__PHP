Vaults / The source watch

The source watch: every outside source on php2shell, watched and snapshotted

A vault that does one thing. It watches every outside source on the story, 913 pages from 597 publishers in 59 countries, and on each run records what access it had and keeps a new snapshot whenever the content changed: the text, a screenshot, the HTML as served and as rendered, the headers and the sha256 of each, and a graph of what the page says and what changed since the last look. A small, focused Wayback Machine whose diffs are graphs. Run by researcher@php.newsroom; public, read-only.

Raw and analysis are separate trees

Raw is what was served; analysis is what the watcher made of it, and it can be regenerated from raw plus the vocabularies plus the tools. That rule is how you tell the two apart. A snapshot in raw/ is never edited or deleted: a new look at the page makes a new folder, and only when something changed. Every check, changed or not, is still a line in checks/ with its status, access and hashes.

What we can and cannot read

Every check classifies the answer it got. A page the watcher cannot read stays on the list: the challenge page's hash is the record, and the gap is published.

AccessPagesMeans
open735Served the content to an anonymous browser.
paywalled15Subscription press we hold no licence for. Either it served a subscription or registration wall instead of the content, or (for a publisher marked paywall in its page.json) it refused the anonymous browser with 401 or 403 before any wall. Counted apart from bot-challenge since 22 October 2026.
bot-challenge59Served a bot or browser challenge (Cloudflare, Akamai, DataDome, PerimeterX, Vercel). The challenge page's hash is kept as the record.
login-wall0Wants a signed-in account (some social posts).
geo-blocked0Refused because of where the request came from.
gone8404 or 410: the page no longer exists at this address.
down615xx, a timeout, a DNS failure or a refused connection: the site is not answering. For the affected sites this is the finding.
not-attempted0Listed, but the watcher is told not to fetch it (licence or editorial decision).

Graphs: one grammar at every altitude

Every graph in the vault uses the grammar in taxonomy/verbs.json (22 verbs): an edge is a verb with a named inverse, and there is no generic association edge (graphs.sgit.ai). The world graph alone has 1620 nodes and 3219 edges. Entities (a CVE id, a PHP version, an organisation with this site's actor ids, a term with its translations) are where the altitudes join, and where the vault joins this site. What changed on a page is answered by a query over the change section, not by comparing two screenshots; the extractor is deterministic.

AltitudeWhat it describes
worldcountry, sector, publisher, page, language: where the watched pages sit (index/world.json)
watchrun, check, page, access, snapshot: what the watcher did and saw (runs/**/graph.json)
documentsnapshot, block, entity: what one snapshot of a page says (graph/**/<UTC>.json)
changechange, block, entity: what moved between two snapshots (graph/**/<UTC>.json, section change)

Tips

A tip is a claim that reached the newsroom from outside, recorded with how it arrived, what it says, the pages added to watch it, and what has to be checked before anything on this site relies on it. Its status uses this site's words.

Tip 2026-10-22/001 · received 2026-10-22T14:38:00+02:00 · reported

Their browser extension, php2shell-checker, was just approved on the Chrome Web Store. It checks whether a website runs outdated PHP or a supported PHP application such as WordPress. They describe it as privacy friendly and read-only, with nothing leaving the browser, and aimed at CISOs in the SME segment as easier than running scripts.

Watched as us/tool/chrome-web-store/php2shell-checker. To check: The publisher named on the store listing, and whether it matches who made the claim; The permissions the listing and the manifest ask for, against 'read-only, nothing leaves the browser'; The version history and user count, run by run; Whether it detects the fixed versions (8.5.12 and the backports) correctly.

This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault.
