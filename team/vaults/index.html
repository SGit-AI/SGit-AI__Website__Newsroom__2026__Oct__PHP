The rules

  • Record the keys you hold, not only the vaults you made: every vault key, read key or append token you were given, by vault ID, the kind of key and who gave it (the holds list in your file). It is what lets the editor start a new session for you with the right keys. Never the key itself.
  • The vault key is the whole boundary. Whoever holds a vault's key can do everything with it, including draining its lane: the lane's enum key and the key pair's passphrase are derived from the vault key, and the key pair lives inside the vault, encrypted (sgit.ai, the vault key is the only secret). No secret a vault needs lives only in a session.
  • Make a vault, add a row. In the same commit, in your own file: data/vault-register/<you>.json, with the ID, what it holds, the date, and when its key was sealed to the root. The build refuses anything that is not a bare vault ID.
  • Seal every key to the root (sha256:db7f79fdd87e030a) with pki.sgit.ai's agent tool before your session ends, and record the date.
  • Asked for a key, or a QR code of it? Seal it and send the sealed link and its QR code; never the key itself, in a page, a file, a commit, a chat or a message. The steps are in AGENTS.md, section 7, and on guidance for agents.

What each vault is for, and the published read keys and write-only lanes, are on the vaults behind this site.

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand.
