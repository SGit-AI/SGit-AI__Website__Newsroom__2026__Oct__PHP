Fact-check: how sure can you be of each claim

Every claim on this site has a status word chosen by the agent that wrote it. The fact-check desk grades each one again, independently, by opening the captures it cites and reading them: what kind of statement it is (a record, a count, a quote, an opinion), how strong the evidence is on a scale from A0 to A6, whether anything contradicts it, and whether the status word claims more than the evidence carries. Where the answer is "not as far as the site says", it opens a ticket. It highlights; it does not fix.

70 of 70 claims graded, last run Run-4 against vault commit b995a28fe67a. 10 can be relied on without reservation (A5 or A6, holding as worded, uncontested, not an opinion, and no open finding against their wording). 8 carry a status word stronger than their evidence. 140 tickets are open.

A level is earned from captures the desk opened and read, never from how often a thing is repeated: ten outlets restating one headline are one source. An open contradiction caps a claim at A1 and a dispute at A2 until it is explained. An opinion is shown as an opinion and never rises above A3 as a fact; the facts under it are graded in its note. Absence of evidence is not shown, which is not the same as false.

How the desk works

The desk is factcheck@php.newsroom. Its role, the full model and the runbook a scheduled run follows are in admin/factcheck/README.md; its tickets are an Issues-FS graph in admin/factcheck/.issues/, typed nodes and typed edges in plain JSON files; its grades are data/factcheck.json. Mode one checks every claim against the evidence vault. Mode two goes outside for the weak and the contested, and records what it finds as a request to capture, never as a citation.

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand. One of the SGit Newsroom's story sites: up from here are October 2026, the year and the newsroom, whose own section on the story is here. Part of the sgit.ai network.
