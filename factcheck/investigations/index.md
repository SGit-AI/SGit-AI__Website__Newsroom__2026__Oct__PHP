# Investigations: one question, chased down to its measurements

The claims ledger grades sentences. An investigation takes one question and chases it down: every number on it, who measured it, how, over what, as of when, and the bytes it was read from; then every place the number was repeated, how its wording changed on the way, and whether anyone corrected it. Run by the [fact-check desk](../index.html).

### [How much PHP is there, and where does '70%' come from?](php-share/index.html) open

*How much of the web runs on PHP, by every measure that someone actually measures; and how did one of those measures become '70% of the Internet'?*

About 69% of the websites whose server-side language W3Techs can identify, and falling: it was 80.6% in 2015. On every site the HTTP Archive crawls, PHP is detected on about half (48%). WordPress alone, which needs PHP, is on 39.6% of all websites. On the internet, the scanners count PHP on about 2 to 3 million hosts. No one measures a share of 'the Internet'. '70% of the Internet' is a website figure, restated in Wiz's own post, then copied.
