# How much PHP is there, and where does '70%' come from?

How much of the web runs on PHP, by every measure that someone actually measures; and how did one of those measures become '70% of the Internet'? An investigation by the [fact-check desk](../../index.html), opened 2026-10-24 at the editor's request; as of 2026-10-24T01:50Z.

## The short answer

**About 69% of the websites whose server-side language W3Techs can identify, and falling: it was 80.6% in 2015. On every site the HTTP Archive crawls, PHP is detected on about half (48%). WordPress alone, which needs PHP, is on 39.6% of all websites. On the internet, the scanners count PHP on about 2 to 3 million hosts. No one measures a share of 'the Internet'. '70% of the Internet' is a website figure, restated in Wiz's own post, then copied.**

## The versions of the claim, graded

On the desk's spectrum of evidence, from A0 (nothing carries it) to A6 (the primary record carries it).
- **A6** Established●●●●●● ***PHP runs about 69-70% of the websites whose server-side language is known*** · Count · holdsW3Techs' own page, fetched on 24 October: 69.3% as of 23 October, on 'all the websites whose server-side programming language we know'. The measurer is the primary record of its own measurement. It is one measurer, and W3Techs does not publish what share of sites have a known language.
- **A1** Weak signal●○○○○○ ***PHP runs 70% of all websites*** · Count · holds in partNo measurer of all websites gives 70%. The one that counts every crawled site, the HTTP Archive, detects PHP on 48.3% of mobile origins (September 2026, the desk's ratio of two published counts); WordPress alone is 39.6% of all sites (W3Techs). 'Of all websites' drops W3Techs' qualifier; W3Techs' own PHP 8 page does the same. The truth is between about 40% and about 70%, depending on what the unknown-language sites run.
- **A0** Unsupported○○○○○○ ***70% of the Internet*** · Count · not shownNothing measures a share of 'the Internet'. Scanners count PHP on about 2 to 3 million internet-facing hosts (Censys, VulnCheck, Shodan), 5.1 million IP addresses (VirusTotal). The phrase first appears in Wiz's X post at 13:32 UTC on 21 October; the body of Wiz's own blog post says 'websites'. It is a website share restated, a change of denominator, not a measurement.
- **A2** Reported●●○○○○ ***75% of websites (Reuters, 'according to W3Techs')*** · Count · holds in partW3Techs' yearly table gives 75.2% on 1 January 2025, 72.4% on 1 January 2026 and 69.3% on 23 October 2026. Reuters' 75% matches the January 2025 figure, not the one W3Techs showed when Reuters wrote: an out-of-date number, correctly attributed.
- **A0** Unsupported○○○○○○ ***could take down 70% of the internet (Fortune)*** · Opinion · not shownFortune's headline and lede, in its own voice, with no source: a usage share became a share that could be taken down. Its only attributed 70% is an accurate quote of Wiz's conclusion. Nothing in the evidence measures what could be taken down.

## What the evidence shows
- The measurement exists, and it is W3Techs: 69.3% of the websites whose server-side programming language W3Techs knows, on 23 October 2026. Every outlet that names a measurer names W3Techs.
- It is falling, and has been for a decade: 80.6% in January 2015, 75.2% in January 2025, 72.4% in January 2026, 69.3% now. '70%' is already a rounding up.
- The bigger the site, the less PHP: 64.4% of the top million, 60.9% of the top 100,000, 58.7% of the top 1,000 (W3Techs, known-language sites).
- On all websites, the only measurer that counts every crawled site gives about half: the HTTP Archive detects PHP on 48.3% of mobile origins and 47.3% of desktop origins in September 2026, down from about 58% in January 2023.
- WordPress, which cannot run without PHP, is on 39.6% of all websites (W3Techs): a floor for PHP's share of all sites in that sample.
- Counted in sites rather than shares: BuiltWith 23.96 million live PHP sites, Wappalyzer about 22.7 million. Neither publishes a denominator.
- On the internet, scanners count hosts, not shares: about 2.6 million (Censys, VulnCheck), 1.9 million (Shodan), 5.1 million IP addresses (VirusTotal). Palo Alto's 22.6 million 'instances' (via Fortune) is not in Palo Alto's own advisory and is ten times every other count.
- In the evidence vault, 127 statements of PHP's share in 11 languages, from 94 sources: 39 against 'the Internet' or 'the web', 29 against all websites, 30 with W3Techs' known-language denominator, 23 with no denominator.
- '70% of the Internet' starts with Wiz: its X post at 13:32 UTC on 21 October, then its blog headline and conclusion. The same post's executive summary says 'powers 70% of all websites in the world'. The denominator changes inside the origin, which cites no measurement.
- 17 statements link a measurement; only one of the 39 'Internet' statements does (MediaNama puts a W3Techs link on the words '70% of the internet').
- Re-checked live on 24 October: 73 page records at 68 addresses; 64 unchanged, 4 reworded without a note, 3 blocked, 2 could not be checked, none corrected. Wiz has changed nothing. CNN rewrote its 70% lines twice and still says 'internet' in the headline.
- Outside the 70%: AWS rewrote 'PHP2Shell is now being broadly exploited in the wild' as 'now seeing broad exploitation attempts in the wild', without a note. This site quotes the first wording.

## Fortune: 'could take down 70% of the internet'

The editor asked for this one (UF-005.1, board 195). Fortune's article by Emily Forlini, Senior AI Reporter, 2026-10-21T18:48:38Z (datePublished); modified 2026-10-22T14:56:06Z; captured 2026-10-23T01:20Z, as captured in the vault:
- **h1:** *AI tools discovered the PHP vulnerability that could take down 70% of the internet. Now, the race is on to patch it before AI-powered hackers attack*
- **html title:** *AI helped discover the PHP vulnerability that could take down 70% of the internet | Fortune*
- **lede:** *Today, the defenders are hoping they got a leg up in identifying a critical issue that stands to take down 70% of the internet if left unaddressed.*
- **quote:** *“With 70% of the internet affected, the impact of php2shell cannot be overstated,” Wiz said in a blog post announcing its findings.*
- **other share sentences:** *Most web pages run using an open-source programming language called PHP.* *Among the most well known sites that run on PHP is WordPress, which powers roughly 40% of all websites, according to Reuters.*

**What it credits.** The 70% figure is attributed only through the quote of Wiz's conclusion, linked to the Wiz blog. The headline and lede state it in Fortune's own voice with no source. Fortune's one W3Techs link (anchor 'according to') supports a list of example PHP sites (Microsoft.com, Wikipedia.org, Archive.org, TheGuardian.com, Baidu.com), not the share; the WordPress 40% is credited to Reuters.

**Against Wiz.** Fortune quotes the Wiz conclusion accurately (only 'Internet' to 'internet' and 'PHP2Shell' to 'php2shell'). But its headline and lede change Wiz's verb: Wiz wrote 'affecting 70% of the Internet' and 'With 70% of the Internet affected'; Fortune wrote 'could take down 70% of the internet' and 'stands to take down 70% of the internet if left unaddressed'. Usage share becomes the share that could be knocked offline. Fortune does not use Wiz's other wording, 'powers 70% of all websites in the world'.

**Against W3Techs.** W3Techs measures PHP on 69.3% (21 Oct, as cited by Tenable, Bitpage, AI4Business, Critic Arad, BDM, Marketing4eCommerce, TechTarget Japan) of websites whose server-side programming language it can identify: a usage share among sites with a detectable language, not of all websites, not of 'the internet', and not of exposure. Fortune links W3Techs but not for this figure, keeps no qualifier, and turns the denominator into 'the internet' and the measure into what could be 'taken down'. Its body does carry a softer qualitative line ('Most web pages run using ... PHP').

**Between its own versions.** H1 'AI tools discovered...' versus /og:title 'AI helped discover...': the AI claim is softened between headline versions; the 70% wording stays.
